The first article from Disconnected has been published in *Personality and Individual Differences*. Jenna Pfeifer and colleagues studied whether young adults who feel lonely pay more attention to images of social threat, such as being excluded. The idea is common in the literature, but earlier findings contradict each other.

More than 240 TU Delft students took part. They viewed four photos at a time while an eye tracker recorded their eye movements. The photos were made with generative AI, so that the researchers could control exactly what they showed: social or non-social, positive or negative.

The researchers found no association between loneliness and attention to socially threatening images. Heightened vigilance for social threat therefore does not appear to be a feature of loneliness in young adults without clinical complaints.

Read the [article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2025.113415). The data and analysis code are on [4TU.ResearchData](https://doi.org/10.4121/b02ace68-8e95-4355-bcea-f073c52e8a14).
