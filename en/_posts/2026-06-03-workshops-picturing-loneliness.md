What does loneliness look like? Jenna Pfeifer developed a workshop in which participants describe a fictional "young adult who might be lonely" and have an AI image generator make a picture of it. Those who are not satisfied revise the description and try again. The chosen images go into a shared gallery and are the starting point for a group discussion. The method is derived from photo-elicitation, in which photos start a conversation about topics people do not easily talk about.

Three workshops took place between February and June 2026, with 19 participants in total: at TU Delft, at Fontys in Eindhoven, and at Studium Generale in Delft. Together they placed 53 images in the gallery.

Participants described loneliness more often as a lack of connection among other people than as being alone: someone in a group or at a party, without contact with the people around them. The images broadly followed those descriptions, but the image generator often added details nobody had asked for, such as an averted gaze, a withdrawn posture, or grey weather.

The workshop code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/jennapfeifer/loneliness-workshop).
