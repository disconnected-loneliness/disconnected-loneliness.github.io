At what moment does a reader notice that a character in a story starts to feel lonely? Bianca Sutcliffe developed a way to measure this without interrupting the reading. Short stories appear on the screen word by word. Participants press a key at the moment they feel the character's state changes, or hold the key for as long as that state lasts. The states were loneliness, positive or negative mood, and uncertainty.

The online experiment had 1,606 participants. In the parts of a story where the intended state applied, participants pressed the key 3.8 times as often as in the parts with the opposite state. Those asked to hold the key did so across 62.9% of the parts with the intended state, against 17.6% of the parts with the opposite state.

The measurements serve as a human reference for follow-up research, in which AI models read the same texts.

The manuscript has been submitted to a journal. The [preprint](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.28898.49602), the [data](https://doi.org/10.4121/0f103cfd-9c40-488c-906f-1e401531da40), and the [experiment code](https://github.com/beeeeeyonktu/affective_state_updating_tracker) are public.
