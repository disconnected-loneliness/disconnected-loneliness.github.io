Readers follow how a character's feelings change as a narrative unfolds. Measuring this during reading usually requires a separate rating scale on the screen, which can compete with the text. Bianca Sutcliffe compared two ways of doing this with a single key: pressing the key when the character moves towards a given state, and holding the key for as long as that state seems present. The states were loneliness, valence (positive or negative), and uncertainty.

In an online experiment, 1,606 participants read narratives that appeared word by word. Presses were 3.8 times as frequent in the parts of a narrative with the target state as in the parts with the opposite state. Participants held the key across 62.9% of the target-state parts, compared with 17.6% of the opposite-state parts.

These data provide a human reference against which AI models reading the same texts will be evaluated.

The manuscript is under review. The [preprint](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.28898.49602), the [data](https://doi.org/10.4121/0f103cfd-9c40-488c-906f-1e401531da40), and the [experiment code](https://github.com/beeeeeyonktu/affective_state_updating_tracker) are public.
