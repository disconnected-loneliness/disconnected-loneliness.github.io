Disconnected studies the relationship between technology use and loneliness among young adults aged 18 to 26. Technology can increase feelings of loneliness, but it can also reduce them. The project examines short-, medium-, and long-term effects, as well as the taboo surrounding loneliness. Its starting point is that social presence plays a key role: the extent to which a person experiences someone else as present, ranging from abstract communication on social platforms to face-to-face contact.

Disconnected is a collaboration between TU Delft and Amsterdam UMC, with partner organisations outside academia. The project runs from 2024 to 2029 and is funded by NWO through the Loneliness programme of the Dutch Research Agenda (NWA), project number NWA.1572.21.012, Grant ID [https://doi.org/10.61686/QWHDT95661](https://doi.org/10.61686/QWHDT95661).

## Lines of research

**Technology and loneliness** (Bianca Sutcliffe, TU Delft). What are the immediate effects of current and emerging technologies, such as AI, augmented reality, and social robots, on loneliness? The first studies address how well AI models recognise loneliness and other emotional states in language, compared with people.

**Cognitive and emotional mechanisms** (Jenna Pfeifer, TU Delft). Which cognitive and emotional processes are involved when young adults use technology over longer periods, and how does the taboo around loneliness contribute to social isolation? The first studies address how young adults perceive and judge social situations, and ways to make loneliness easier to discuss.

**Neuroscience of technology use** (Daphne Boucherie, Amsterdam UMC). How are technology use and loneliness related to brain structure and activity? This line combines analyses of existing large MRI datasets with new measurements.

**Joint longitudinal study** (Daphne Boucherie and Jenna Pfeifer). The cognitive and neuroscience lines are working together on a study that combines behavioural measures and brain scans. A screening study among young adults maps how they perceive and interpret social information. A smaller group is then followed for about a year with repeated MRI sessions, to examine how stable the association between loneliness and the brain is.

**Scalability and impact** (project team and partners). What do the results mean for education, care, technology development, and policy? This line brings together the results of the other lines and discusses their implications with the partners and other stakeholders.

## How we work

**Technology as a research instrument.** In the project, technology is not only a subject of research but also an instrument. We use generative AI to create images and texts for our experiments. This also lets us show situations that are hard to find in existing image databases, such as realistic depictions of social exclusion.

**Open.** We make articles, data, code, and preregistrations public. See the [publications]({{ '/en/publications/' | relative_url }}).

**With partners.** We work with NVO, NewHealth, 8D, LSVb, and the TU Delft psychologists. See the [partners]({{ '/en/partners/' | relative_url }}).
