Loneliness is common among young adults aged 18 to 26. This generation grew up with smartphones and social media, and now also talks to AI chatbots. Technology can connect people, but it can also replace contact. In Disconnected we study what technology does to loneliness, for better and for worse.

Disconnected is a collaboration between TU Delft and Amsterdam UMC, with partners from practice. The project runs from 2024 to 2029 and is funded by NWO through the Loneliness programme of the Dutch Research Agenda (NWA), project number NWA.1572.21.012, Grant ID [https://doi.org/10.61686/QWHDT95661](https://doi.org/10.61686/QWHDT95661).

## Three lines of research

**Language.** How do people pick up emotions, and changes in them, from text and speech? And what do language models make of them? This is the research of Bianca Sutcliffe (TU Delft).

**Images.** How do young adults picture loneliness, how do they recognise it in someone else, and how do they respond to advice from AI? This is the research of Jenna Pfeifer (TU Delft).

**Brain.** What happens in the brain when someone is lonely? Daphne Boucherie (Amsterdam UMC) studies this with brain scans, and is preparing a study that follows young adults for a year.

## How we work

**AI as a research instrument.** We often make the images and texts in our experiments with generative AI. This lets us control exactly what participants see or read.

**Open.** We make articles, data, code, and preregistrations public. See the [publications]({{ '/en/publications/' | relative_url }}).

**With practice.** We work with NVO, NewHealth, 8D, LSVb, and the student psychologists of TU Delft. See the [partners]({{ '/en/partners/' | relative_url }}).
